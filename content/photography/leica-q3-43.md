> **Draft brief — not for publication.** Fill the prompts with real, specific detail —
> bullet points, half-sentences, or a voice-memo transcript pasted in raw are all fine.
> Then a Claude Code session drafts the piece in the house voice and you edit it.
> If a prompt has no true answer, leave it blank. Nothing gets invented.

> Existing seed line, keep if true: “When you need to get the shot, but not so quickly you have to use your phone”

## The person

- Whose camera or lens is this, and what do their days look like?
- Why do we care about them before we care about the camera or lens?

## History

- When did it arrive, and what was happening in their life at the time? (Not the acquisition story — what came after.)
- What has it been through since? Places, trips, incidents, seasons. Name them.

## The unique detail

- One thing that happened to THIS camera or lens that could not belong to anyone else's.
  The scratch, the repair, the border crossing, the day it failed. This is the piece's spine — without it the draft fails.

## Use

- How is it actually used, day to day? What surprised them about living with it?
- What is worn, faded, loose, or broken in? Be exact — where a thumb sits, which corner hits the ground.

## The unobvious angle

- What does every other writer say about this camera or lens? What is the thing nobody thought to look at?

## Photos

- Which photos exist in the library, and what still needs shooting? In use or after use. Never on white. The scratch visible.
