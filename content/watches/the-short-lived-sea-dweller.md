Somewhere on the left side of the case is a small valve that has, in all probability, never once been needed by the person wearing it. It exists for saturation divers — men who live for weeks in pressurised chambers breathing helium, whose watches used to blow their own crystals off during decompression as the gas fought its way out. Rolex and the commercial diving world solved this in the late 1960s with a one-way escape valve, and the Sea-Dweller has carried it ever since: a complication for a job measured in atmospheres, worn to meetings.

The reference 116600 — the Sea-Dweller 4000 — is the shortest chapter in that story. Introduced in 2014, gone by 2017. Three years is nothing in Rolex time; references usually run a decade or more. It arrived as a correction (the Sea-Dweller had been dropped from the catalogue entirely in 2008, to general dismay) and it was, by the account of most people who handled one, exactly right: forty millimetres, ceramic bezel, no date magnifier, the dial clean and symmetrical the way the original 1967 watch was. Then Basel 2017, and its replacement: forty-three millimetres, with the cyclops lens over the date — the first Sea-Dweller ever to wear one.

Which made the 116600 the last of something. The watch photographed here is its predecessor, the 16600, wearing the same plain crystal; between them they close out a fifty-year run of Sea-Dwellers that kept the dial glass flat and uninterrupted. Collectors noticed the arithmetic — a three-year reference at the end of a fifty-year line — and the 116600 quietly became the one to find.

There is a lesson in it that has nothing to do with dive tables. The 116600 was discontinued, near as anyone can tell, for being too close to the Submariner — too correct, too familiar, insufficiently distinct on a shelf where every watch must justify its slot. The qualities that ended it are the qualities people now hunt it for.

The valve, meanwhile, waits on every example, rated to a depth of four thousand feet, ready for a decompression that is never coming.
